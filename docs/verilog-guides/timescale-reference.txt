1. WHAT IS `timescale`? (CONCEPT & ANALOGY)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Think of `timescale as setting the rules for a stopwatch when running a simulation in Verilog.
When you write `#5` in code, the simulator does not automatically know if you mean 5 seconds, 5 minutes, or 5 nanoseconds.
`timescale defines two essential parameters:

  1. Time Unit      : How long 1 simulation tick (#1) lasts.
  2. Time Precision : The smallest sub-tick time interval before rounding occurs.

- Syntax Anatomy:
  `timescale <time_unit> / <time_precision>
  Example: `timescale 1ns / 1ps
           - 1ns  = Time Unit (what #1 actually stands for)
           - 1ps  = Time Precision (smallest tick your stopwatch can display)

- Everyday Baking Analogy:
  * Time Unit (1 Minute)    : Recipe says "bake for #10" -> Bakes for 10 minutes.
  * Time Precision (1 Sec)  : Timer measures down to seconds. Setting timer to 10 min 0.4 sec rounds to 10 min 0 sec.


2. THE 3 GOLDEN RULES OF `timescale
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Rule 1 (Allowed Multipliers) : Only 1, 10, or 100 can precede the time unit (e.g., 1ns, 10ns, 100ns). `5ns` is ILLEGAL.
  Rule 2 (Allowed Time Units)  : s (seconds), ms (milliseconds), us (microseconds), ns (nanoseconds), 
                                 ps (picoseconds), fs (femtoseconds).
  Rule 3 (Precision Constraint): Precision MUST be less than or equal to Unit. `timescale 1ps / 1ns is ILLEGAL.


3. VERILOG PRECISION ROUNDING EXAMPLE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`timescale 1ns / 1ps   // Unit = 1 ns, Precision = 1 ps (0.001 ns = 3 decimal places)

module tb_example;
    reg clk;
    initial begin
        clk = 0;
        #5.1238 clk = 1; 
        // 1. #5.1238 = 5.1238 nanoseconds.
        // 2. Precision = 1ps (0.001ns), rounding to 3 decimal places.
        // 3. clk toggles HIGH at exactly 5.124 ns.
    end
endmodule


4. WHY WE CONVERT TO SECONDS (EXPLANATION OF 10^-9)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Meaning of Nano (n):
  * In SI metric system, nano (n) means one-billionth (10^-9).
  * 1 nanosecond (ns) = 1 / 1,000,000,000 second = 10^-9 seconds (s).

- Why Conversion is Mandatory for Frequency:
  * The unit of frequency is Hertz (Hz), defined strictly as cycles per second (s^-1):
    1 Hz = 1 / (1 second)
  * To apply the frequency formula f = 1 / T, period T MUST be expressed in standard seconds (s), not nanoseconds (ns).

- Standard Frequency Unit (Hertz):
  * Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz = cycles per second).
  * Formula: f = 1 / T
  * Period (T) MUST be converted into standard seconds (s) before calculating frequency in Hz.

- Unit Multipliers Reference:
  * Micro (us) : 10^-6 s
  * Nano  (ns) : 10^-9 s  (1 ns = 0.000000001 s)
  * Pico  (ps) : 10^-12 s


5. SOLVED PRACTICE QUESTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[QUESTION 1]
Assume `timescale 1ns/100ps. Find the period and frequency of the clock. [2 marks]
Code:
   initial begin
       clk = 1'b0;
       forever #10 clk = ~clk;
   end

Solution:
   - Time Unit = 1 ns
   - Half-Period (t_half) = 10 units = 10 * 1 ns = 10 ns
   - Clock Period (T) = 2 * t_half = 2 * 10 ns = 20 ns
   - Frequency (f) = 1 / T = 1 / (20 * 10^-9 s) = 50,000,000 Hz = 50 MHz
   Answers: Clock Period (T) = 20 ns | Clock Frequency (f) = 50 MHz

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[QUESTION 2]
Assume `timescale 1ns/1ps. Find the clock period and frequency. [2 marks]
Code:

   end

Solution:
   - Time Unit = 1 ns
   - Half-Period (t_half) = 7.5 units = 7.5 * 1 ns = 7.5 ns
   - Clock Period (T) = 2 * t_half = 2 * 7.5 ns = 15 ns
   - Frequency (f) = 1 / T = 1 / (15 * 10^-9 s) = 66,666,666.67 Hz ≈ 66.67 MHz
   Answers: Clock Period (T) = 15 ns | Clock Frequency (f) = 66.67 MHz
